04 / Inside the actual apps

Three apps.
One referral journey.

Real screens from the running prototype: the iPad kiosk where a person starts, the doctor’s iPhone app that verifies a referral, and the clinician web workspace.

Explore the web workspace ↗
iPad

The community kiosk

Private intake in six languages, by form or by voice. It finishes with a referral sealed by a signature and a four-digit code. No name or account is needed.

iPhone

The doctor’s verification app

Anonymous referrals arrive with a language, specialty and demo priority. At the visit, the patient re-enters their code and signature on the doctor’s phone. The device then decrypts and verifies the referral, and it can be used only once.

Works offline from a cached referral and reconciles when back online. Signature matching is demonstration logic, not identity assurance.

Web

The clinician workspace

Filter and prioritise submitted histories. It also offers county-level equity actions and an access-planning digital twin.

Screenshots captured from the running kiosk, doctor and web apps on 15 November 2026 using synthetic demo records only. Signature matching and priority scores are unvalidated demonstration logic. Public-data displays and simulations have their own methods and limits in the app. Do not enter real patient information into the demo.

05 / Built on evidence. Ready for evaluation.

A clear need.
A testable proposition.

Research informs the design. A clinical pilot must establish what this specific service can deliver.

Established need

Earlier access matters.

Finland’s strategy calls for accessible health communication and easier access to early assessment. Its 2035 target is at least 70% of cancers diagnosed at stages I–II.

Finnish strategy · A future target ↗
Component research

The format has precedent.

A scoping review included 134 primary studies of health kiosks, with 32 involving community settings. This supports investigating the delivery channel.

Maramba et al., 2022 ↗
Proposed evaluation

Measure the whole journey.

Does the service help people reach appropriate assessment? We propose measuring completed care, missed urgent concerns, language performance and full pathway costs.

Explore the evaluation plan ↗
What has been demonstrated, and what still needs validation?

A synthetic prototype

The repository includes kiosk and patient intake, a referral wallet, clinician verification, a web workspace, and shared demo appointment booking. Signature matching and priority scores are demonstration logic. No real clinic booking service is connected.

A supervised pilot proposal

Clinical safety, language accuracy, identity assurance and outcome improvements need validation. A virtual-interviewer experiment supports a possible disclosure benefit, not proof of earlier diagnosis.

Lucas et al., 2014 ↗
How do records, codes and the proposed blockchain fit in?

The prototype demonstrates a referral wallet and signature-plus-code verification. These do not establish anonymity or secure identity assurance. A production access and recovery process needs further work. The pilot proposes provider-approved encrypted storage; any blockchain audit layer would be evaluated separately, with identifiable clinical content kept off-chain.
